A LOCKED ROOM OF GOLD

First, a little history.

Picture a king's treasury. In this simplified account of [[mercantilism]], many European governments measured wealth in [[bullion]] and treated one nation's gain as another's loss. They guarded trade routes, taxed imports and tried to keep wealth at home.

Over time, a different understanding of wealth creation gained ground: production, exchange and new demand could expand what was available. This is a model for the comparison, not a complete history of early modern economics.

Historical primer written for the demo. The analogy's limits matter throughout this guide.

THE SAME QUESTION, A NEW RESOURCE

What if we treat
intelligence like gold?

Venkatesh Rao calls this [[cognitive mercantilism|cognitive-mercantilism]]. Nathaniel Whittemore extends the discussion to demand: easier access to expertise may create uses we could not previously pursue.

FROM THE ANALOGY TO THE CLASSROOM

Not every limit
is hoarding.

A policy can protect people and still distribute access unequally. Examine both.

⌑ Guardrail
A limit with a named protective purpose, a specific harm and a proportionate scope.
▣ Gate
A status-based limit whose protective rationale has not been established.

Original workshop framing. The questions support judgment; they do not validate a policy.
