# Scarcity or Access?

**Cognitive Mercantilism and AI in Schools**

A concept for an offline workshop tool that helps educators discuss AI access, protective limits, and the thinking students need to develop. It draws on Venkatesh Rao's cognitive-mercantilism analogy and Nathaniel Whittemore's commentary, then applies an original three-question discussion aid to fictional school scenarios.

## The discussion aid

1. Who or what does this limit protect?
2. From what specific harm?
3. Would a smaller limit do the same job?

These questions support discussion. The tool is illustrative, and the proposed classifications are prompts for reasoning. They are not a validated policy instrument or findings about any particular district. Learning goals, access inequities, implementation capacity, and residual risk also matter.

## Proposed implementation

The original specification calls for plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with local script-loaded data, five views, keyboard access, a glossary, and light/dark themes. It should open directly from a local file and work offline. Scenario choices would remain in memory; display preferences alone could use local storage. No analytics, external assets, or collection of participant or student information is planned.

## Sources and caveats

The complete draft references are in [CONCEPT.md](CONCEPT.md#references). Verified source pointers and their review status are in [SOURCE_CHECKS.md](SOURCE_CHECKS.md).

The analogy offers a way to question exclusionary assumptions. It does not establish that increased AI use improves learning, that every restriction reflects hoarding, or that historical economic change predicts educational outcomes. The history of increased labor and consumption is contested and entangled with coercion and colonial extraction; those qualifications are integral to the proposal.
