# Concept review

Written on: mac · codex · November 11, 2026

Scope: review of the supplied concept and preparation of a repository. The original `CONCEPT.md` is preserved unchanged. All replacement language below is proposed for Micah's review, not an accepted revision or a completed build.

## Summary assessment

The concept is a coherent workshop proposal. It introduces an unfamiliar analogy, moves into recognizable school decisions, and preserves distinctions among quotations, paraphrases, and original framing. Its main weakness is that the guardrail/gate distinction sometimes becomes a verdict about a decision when the evidence supports only a question about that decision's purpose and effects.

Proceed with the concept, after tightening that distinction and correcting the source details below. The idea is ready for a scoped prototype once those content decisions are resolved; the current package is not presentation-ready or a validated policy instrument.

## What is working

- The three questions in section 1.5 make an abstract argument usable in a room of educators. They connect a restriction to a protected interest, a specific harm, and proportionality.
- Source labels and the source-status table make the provenance unusually visible. Preserve the distinction between the draft author's reading and checks performed in this review.
- The historical dispute, colonial extraction, manufactured scarcity, and limits of increased demand are already part of the argument. They should appear beside the optimistic prediction, as specified, rather than only at the end.
- The unaided-writing scenario supplies an instructional reason for a limit. It prevents the workshop from reducing every decision to tool adoption.
- The proposed static architecture fits offline conference use. Local script tags, system fonts, and in-memory scenario choices keep the technical scope manageable.

## Primary concerns

### 1. A protective purpose and an inequitable effect can coexist

**Locations:** sections 1.4–1.5; section 2.4's comparison line; Appendix C scenarios 1, 3, 4, 6, and 9.

The draft sometimes treats the absence of an alternative as evidence that a protective restriction has become a gate. A public-chatbot restriction can remain necessary even when an approved alternative is unaffordable. A refusal to automate grade writes can remain proportionate when the district cannot reliably review, monitor, or reverse changes. The inequity or lost opportunity deserves attention without changing the nature of the protected risk.

Similarly, unequal funding is an allocation problem; it need not reflect a belief that intelligence belongs in approved hands. Recognizing real scarcity does not endorse the existing allocation.

**Recommendation:** retain the three calls for discussion, but ask participants separately about the protective rationale and access consequences. Replace the scarcity-to-access scale with three labeled discussion positions. Neither a room vote nor an editorial lean is a measured quantity or an answer key. Add “What information would change your judgment?”

**Proposed replacement for section 1.5's final paragraph:**

> These questions help us examine a limit's purpose and scope. A clear protective rationale supports reading it as a guardrail; an unexplained status requirement raises a question about gatekeeping. A decision can protect people and still distribute access unequally. Missing information, limited capacity, and genuine resource constraints may require a separate judgment.

### 2. Access to AI output and the development of human capability need distinct treatment

**Locations:** sections 1.4 and 1.6; Appendix A; Appendix B belief 4; Appendix E's pedagogical-friction definition.

The argument moves among intelligence, expertise, generated output, and learners' developing capabilities. Those are related but different. The draft recognizes that more use does not establish better thinking, but that qualification needs to inform the positive framing too. An educator can support broad access while requiring independent performance on a particular task.

**Recommendation:** introduce the distinction before the scenarios. For each instructional scenario, identify the thinking to develop, what assistance changes, and what evidence would show learning. Treat the glossary's current pedagogical-friction entry as a narrow illustration of productive friction, rather than a complete definition of the broader construct.

**Proposed insertion after section 1.4:**

> Here, access means an opportunity to use AI support. It does not establish that a learner has developed the knowledge or judgment expressed in the output. Instructional decisions also need to specify which thinking students must practice and demonstrate themselves.

### 3. Contract and account controls reduce particular risks; they do not remove exposure

**Locations:** Appendix C scenarios 1, 3, 5, and 6.

The approved-tool alternative in scenario 1 claims the same drafting job can happen without exposure. Scenario 5 implies that a district account supplies retention limits and an audit trail automatically. Those assurances depend on the actual service, configuration, agreement, permitted data use, and access controls. Read-only gradebook access still exposes records; a human approval step also needs meaningful review and a way to recover from errors.

For FERPA's school-official exception, the U.S. Department of Education identifies conditions including institutional function, direct control, restricted use/redisclosure, and legitimate educational interest. A license alone does not establish those conditions. [Department of Education guidance](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/who-school-official-under-ferpa).

COPPA is also distinct from a vendor's age restriction. FTC guidance describes circumstances in which schools may authorize collection for educational purposes; this does not override vendor terms. [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

**Recommendation:** keep the scenarios, qualify the risk claims, and link their legal summaries to official sources. Treat security, educational suitability, legal permission, and procurement as separate checks.

**Proposed replacement for scenario 1's smaller-limit sentence:**

> A district-approved tool may support this task if its agreement, configuration, and authorized uses permit the necessary data, with only the minimum information disclosed and human review of the draft.

**Proposed replacement for scenario 5's smaller-limit sentence:**

> District-managed accounts can support access control and offboarding; retention, audit logs, and permitted data use still depend on the service and its configuration.

### 4. Provenance is strong in structure but needs a few concrete corrections

**Locations:** section 1.3; sections 1.6–1.7; References; Appendix B belief 3.

- The fixed-demand excerpt attributed to 00:07:00 occurs in the published transcript's **00:08:00 block**. Correct all three occurrences. The separate lump-of-labor excerpt is in the 00:07:00 block. These are transcript block labels, not independently verified audio timings. [Published transcript](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-11-01/transcript.md).
- The missing May episode can now link to the [publisher-provided Apple Podcasts listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-new-jobs-ai-will-create/id1680633614?i=1000767079446). Its [official companion](https://play.aidailybrief.ai/episodes/ai-jobs-and-the-demand-frontier/) presents the six demand dimensions. This review did not listen to the May audio.
- Several Rao excerpts start with an uppercase letter where the original wording follows an introductory word in a sentence. For a strict “exactly as written” acceptance check, record capitalization changes explicitly or choose a complete source sentence. Do not silently normalize quotes in the future data files. [Rao's essay](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/cognitive-mercantilism).
- Describe Humphries and Weisdorf as evidence supporting an industrious-revolution interpretation, while distinguishing that finding from a voluntary-consumption explanation. Their abstract explicitly supports an industrious revolution; it is not simply another rejection of the thesis. [Publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/ej/article/129/623/2867/5490321).
- The abstract-level account in section 1.7 should not be upgraded to a full historical verification. Cambridge's accessible page provides bibliographic metadata and a preview for Clark and Van Der Werf, and says an abstract is unavailable. [Publisher page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/abs/work-in-progress-the-industrious-revolution/E767D221F8C92F3DBB2032CA2AD63228).

The two DOI-bearing references passed registry metadata verification. That result verifies bibliographic consistency, not the historical interpretations or the quoted audio. Detailed coverage is recorded in [SOURCE_CHECKS.md](SOURCE_CHECKS.md).

## Secondary observations

| Location | Observation and proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Section 1.1 and Appendix E, mercantilism | Label the fixed-pile account as a simplified historical model used for this analogy. Add a historical source before presenting it as a general account of several centuries of policy. The framing currently risks turning a heterogeneous history into a single belief. |
| Appendix B, belief 4 | The line about freeing people from farm work feeding them is too categorical, especially beside the colonial-extraction caveat. Delete it or qualify its distributional and historical scope. |
| Appendix E, pedagogical friction | Keep productive and exclusionary friction distinct. Not every difficulty created by withholding a tool is educationally productive. |
| Section 2.4, room vote | Specify that the presenter enters one negotiated room call. The static tool has no shared audience-voting service. Add a “not enough information” discussion prompt without recording participant information. |
| Appendix D, source records | A status such as `read` is ambiguous without who checked it, when, what version, and whether it means full text, transcript, audio, abstract, or metadata. Keep the author's original status separate from this review. Give sources stable IDs and connect excerpts to those IDs. |
| Sections 2.6–2.7, glossary | Generate safe text nodes rather than injecting arbitrary data strings as HTML. A glossary term can occur inside a source label or heading; avoid nested interactive elements and give repeated definitions unique IDs. Escape should return focus to the triggering term. |
| Section 2.7, navigation | Hash navigation with `aria-current` fits page-style views. Use ordinary navigation links unless implementing the complete keyboard and ARIA behavior of a tab widget. Hidden views must also leave the focus order. |
| Section 2.8, validation | Add screen-reader checks, zoom/reflow, disabled-storage behavior, and privacy checks. Test no external requests during ordinary use; source links can open external pages only when deliberately followed. |
| Section 2.3, license | The spec explicitly leaves this to the author. Select licensing for original text and any future code deliberately, with third-party quotations identified. No license is added by this review. |
